Furnace repair & maintenance

Warm, quiet and efficient through an Ontario winter.

The short answer

A gas furnace should be inspected and cleaned once a year, before the heating season. Annual maintenance checks the burners, ignition, flame sensor, heat exchanger, venting, blower and filter, and confirms the furnace is burning safely. It is the simplest way to avoid a mid-winter breakdown and catch carbon monoxide risks early.

Annual furnace maintenance

Our tune-up covers the burners, ignitor and flame sensor, a visual and instrument check of the heat exchanger, venting and draft, gas pressure, blower motor and wheel, condensate drain on high-efficiency units, thermostat operation and the filter. We measure temperature rise against the manufacturer's range and record every reading.

Furnace repair

No heat, short-cycling, a furnace that blows cold air or trips on limit are the common calls. Usually the cause is a dirty flame sensor, failed ignitor, pressure switch, inducer motor or a plugged condensate trap. We diagnose, give you the price before we start, and carry common parts to finish the repair on the first visit.

Replacement and upgrades

Furnaces in many 1990s and early-2000s GTA subdivisions are reaching the end of their 15 to 20 year life. When replacement is the better value, we size the new unit to your home, consider a variable-speed or modulating furnace for comfort, and pair it with your existing or new air conditioner or heat pump.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How often should a furnace be serviced?

Once a year, ideally in September or October before you need the heat.

Why is my furnace blowing cold air?

Common causes are a dirty flame sensor, failed ignitor, a tripped high-limit from a clogged filter, or a blocked condensate drain on a high-efficiency furnace. If resetting the thermostat does not help, call for service.

How long does a furnace last?

Typically 15 to 20 years with annual maintenance. Age alone is not a reason to replace; condition and repair cost are.
