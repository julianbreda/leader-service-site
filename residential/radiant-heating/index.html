Radiant floor heating & snowmelt

Hydronics is a craft, and it's one of ours.

The short answer

Hydronic radiant floor and snowmelt systems should be checked every fall: system pressure, glycol concentration and pH on snowmelt and glycol loops, circulator pumps, mixing valves, zone valves and controls. Balanced and maintained, radiant heat is the quietest and most even heat a home can have. Leader Service maintains these systems in custom homes across the GTA.

Fall commissioning

We check fill pressure and the expansion tank, test glycol freeze protection and pH, purge air, verify each circulator and mixing valve, and confirm every zone calls and satisfies. Snowmelt systems get their sensors and controls tested before the first snowfall.

Balancing and comfort problems

Cold floors, rooms that never reach temperature and noisy pipes usually come from air, poor balancing or a mixing valve that has drifted. We diagnose zone by zone and rebalance the system.

Built for complex homes

Custom and estate homes often combine radiant floors, panel radiators, an indirect tank, snowmelt and pool heat on one plant. We document the whole system so every future visit starts with a clear map of how it works.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How often should a radiant heating system be serviced?

Once a year, in the fall, with glycol tested on snowmelt and glycol loops each season.

Why is one radiant zone cold?

Usually trapped air, a failed zone valve or actuator, a circulator problem or a thermostat fault. Each is checked on a service call.

Do you service snowmelt systems?

Yes, including the boiler or heat source, glycol loop, sensors and controls.
